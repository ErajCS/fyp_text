\begin{abstract}
In Pakistan, expert agronomic knowledge critical to smallholder farmers remains fragmented across informal channels, rendering it undiscoverable at scale. This paper presents the \textbf{PQNK Knowledge Intelligence System}, an AI-powered conversational agent developed in collaboration with \textbf{Pakistan Agriculture Research (PAR)} to democratise \textbf{Paedar Qudratti Nizam-e-Kashtari (PQNK)}, a sustainable natural farming methodology. Our system delivers four core contributions: (1) a fully automated multimodal ingestion pipeline that extracts and indexes diverse PQNK corpus formats---including audio via Faster-Whisper, images via GPT-4o-mini, and documents---into a Qdrant vector store; (2) a strict domain-restricted Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) chatbot that grounds responses exclusively in the curated material, achieving a low 4\% hallucination rate; (3) a bilingual Urdu and English interface with synthesised audio output to lower literacy barriers; and (4) a production-grade role-based knowledge platform. Evaluation shows a mean retrieval precision of 0.80, proving the system a highly replicable blueprint for scaling expert knowledge in low-resource agricultural settings.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Agriculture is the cornerstone of Pakistan's economy, contributing approximately 24\% to GDP
and employing around 35\% of the workforce \cite{pbs2023}. Despite its importance,
smallholder farmers, who cultivate the majority of arable land, remain systematically excluded
from expert agronomic knowledge that could substantially improve their yields, reduce input
costs, and protect soil health.

The \textbf{Paedar Qudratti Nizam-e-Kashtari (PQNK)} methodology, developed by Dr.~Asif
Sharif and operationalised by Pakistan Agriculture Research (PAR), offers a validated
path toward sustainable productivity through natural soil rejuvenation, water conservation,
biological pest management, and seed sovereignty. However, this knowledge is disseminated
purely through direct consultation: in-person sessions, WhatsApp voice messages, and
printed guides. This introduces a fundamental bottleneck, expert time is finite, knowledge
is scattered across incompatible formats (YouTube lectures, PDFs, social media, WhatsApp),
and physical outreach cannot reach remote farming communities at scale.

Large Language Models (LLMs) are often proposed as a solution, yet they introduce critical
risks in specialized domains: general-purpose LLMs hallucinate domain-specific
advice \cite{ji2023survey} recommending, for instance, synthetic fertilizers that PQNK
explicitly prohibits. Moreover, PQNK's internally consistent philosophy is absent from
standard LLM training corpora, making out-of-the-box generation unreliable.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) addresses both issues by anchoring model outputs to a
verified external knowledge base before generation \cite{lewis2020rag}. This paper presents
the \textbf{PQNK Knowledge Intelligence System}: a full-stack, bilingual AI platform that:
(1)~automatically ingests, processes, and vector-indexes heterogeneous PQNK material
through an asynchronous multimodal pipeline; and (2)~delivers expert-grounded, conversational guidance in Urdu and English with audio output.

\subsection*{Problem Statement}
No unified, searchable interface for PQNK knowledge currently exists. Farmers must navigate
disconnected platforms with no cross-corpus query capability. Administrators have no
scalable workflow to process and index newly produced content.

\subsection*{Key Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep, leftmargin=*]
  \item A fully automated 10-stage multimodal ingestion pipeline supporting documents
        (including embedded PDF image extraction), stand-alone images, video lectures,
        and \textbf{audio recordings}.
  \item A domain-restricted hybrid RAG chatbot with bilingual (Urdu/English) text,
        synthesized neural audio output, and clickable source provenance links.
  \item A production-grade role-based knowledge platform integrating React, Flask,
        PostgreSQL, Qdrant, and Google Drive with two-way deletion synchronization.
  \item An invisible audio ingestion pathway that enriches the AI knowledge base without
        exposing audio files in the public repository interface, enabling private corpus
        expansion from expert voice recordings.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Conversational AI in Agriculture}
Early agricultural advisory systems relied on rule-based expert engines and decision trees,
which, while deterministic, lacked natural language flexibility. With the introduction of
Natural Language Understanding (NLU), intent-based chatbots improved query handling.
Mohiuddin et al. \cite{mohiuddin2022} developed a multilingual Urdu chatbot using the Rasa
framework with intent classification trained on manually annotated datasets; however, it
relied on small task-specific corpora, struggled with multi-turn reasoning, and did not
support multimodal content.

\subsection{Large Language Models and Hallucination}
The emergence of instruction-tuned LLMs such as GPT-4 \cite{openai2024} substantially raised
the ceiling of conversational quality. However, as documented in Ji et~al. \cite{ji2023survey},
LLMs frequently generate plausible but factually incorrect content, a critical risk in
high-stakes domains such as agronomy, where incorrect fertilizer or irrigation advice can
result in total crop loss.

\subsection{Retrieval-Augmented Generation}
Lewis et~al. \cite{lewis2020rag} introduced RAG as a principled approach for grounding
LLM outputs within an external document corpus, achieving strong results on knowledge-intensive
tasks. Balaguer et~al. \cite{balaguer2024} demonstrated that RAG systems outperform
fine-tuned LLMs on domain-specific tasks while requiring substantially lower computational
cost, particularly relevant for academic deployments with limited budgets. Feng et~al.
\cite{feng2024} further extended RAG to multimodal modalities by coupling visual detection
models with language generation for plant disease advisory.

\subsection{Multilingual NLP and Speech Recognition}
Nabila et~al. \cite{nabila2022} demonstrated the effectiveness of leveraging Neural Machine
Translation (NMT), specifically the Google Translate API for Urdu domain-specific text,
particularly where custom parallel corpora are unavailable. Radford et~al. \cite{whisper2023}
showed that the Whisper model, trained on 680,000+ hours of multilingual audio via
weak supervision, achieves robust automatic speech recognition across diverse accents,
noise conditions, and languages, making it highly suitable for transcribing informal Urdu
agricultural lecture content.

\subsection{Research Gap}
Existing agricultural AI systems either (i) rely on open-domain knowledge without expert
grounding, (ii) lack multimodal ingestion, (iii) do not support Urdu, or (iv) require
manual indexing. The PQNK Knowledge Intelligence System simultaneously addresses all four
gaps in a single production platform.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{System Architecture Overview}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep, leftmargin=*]

  \item \textbf{Presentation Tier}: React 19 single-page application served via Flask REST~API.

\subsection{Automated Multimodal Ingestion Pipeline}

The central technical contribution is the \textbf{10-stage asynchronous ingestion pipeline}
implemented in \texttt{pipeline\_service.py}. Upon file upload, Flask immediately returns a
success response and spawns an isolated daemon background thread, keeping the UI non-blocking.
Each thread receives a unique UUID workspace to prevent data collisions during concurrent uploads.

\textit{Stage 1 — Google Drive Sync.}
The raw file is uploaded synchronously to the appropriate category folder on Google Drive
(PDFs/, Images/, Videos/, \textbf{Audios/}) using the Drive API v3 with OAuth2.
The \texttt{drive\_file\_id} and \texttt{drive\_view\_link} are written to the PostgreSQL
\texttt{resources} table within this same request-response cycle, guaranteeing that
deletion of a resource from the platform immediately propagates the deletion to Google Drive
(via \texttt{drive\_service.delete\_file()}) regardless of pipeline processing state.

\textit{Stages 2--3 — Content Extraction.}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Documents (Text)}: Text is extracted using Cloud Vision API and then translated by the help of Deep Translator API from Google Translate.
  \item \textbf{Documents (Embedded Images) — new sub-pipeline}: After text extraction, the
        document pipeline also inspects the PDF for embedded diagrams, instructional illustrations,
        and tables using \texttt{extracting\_images\_from\_pdfs.py}. Each page is scanned for
        embedded image objects; decorative images (logos, low-detail graphics) are filtered out
        using pixel standard-deviation thresholds, tables are identified using img2table library and template-matching is done against a logo library and are then described bilingually by GPT-4o-mini
        (\texttt{translating\_images.py}). The resulting \texttt{.txt} files are ingested into
        Qdrant and PostgreSQL alongside the document's textual content. This ensures no
        visual knowledge embedded inside any PDF is left unindexed.

  \item \textbf{Images}: Stand-alone uploaded images follow the same language detection,
        renaming, and GPT-4o-mini bilingual description workflow described above.

  \item \textbf{Audio Recordings ,  new modality}: Expert voice lectures uploaded as
        MP3 or WAV files bypass FFmpeg audio extraction (the file is already audio) and
        are fed directly to Faster-Whisper (large-v3) for transcription. This removes
        the FFmpeg step entirely, halving pipeline latency for pure-audio uploads relative
        to video. Transcripts undergo identical Gemini refinement, bilingual translation,
        and ingestion as video-derived transcripts. Crucially, audio resources are flagged
        \texttt{file\_type = `audio'} in PostgreSQL and are \emph{filtered out} of the
        public \texttt{/api/repository} listing, they enrich the RAG knowledge base
        invisibly, enabling corpus expansion from private expert voice recordings without
        cluttering the repository interface for end users.

  \item \textbf{Videos}: Audio is first extracted to MP3 via FFmpeg. The audio stream is
        then transcribed using \texttt{faster-whisper} (large-v3 variant), a
        CTranslate2-optimised implementation of OpenAI Whisper, running locally on CPU.
        This eliminates per-video API cost, supports Urdu and mixed-language
        lectures, and functions in network-constrained environments. Raw transcripts are
        subsequently refined by the Google GenAI (Gemini) API to correct dialectal
        disfluencies and noise artefacts.
\end{itemize}

\textit{Stage 4 — Language Detection and Bilingual Translation.}
A Unicode character-ratio heuristic identifies Urdu (Arabic-script) versus English tokens.
Urdu text is translated to English using the Deep Translator library (Google Translate NMT
backend). Critically, \textit{both} the original Urdu text and the English translation are
retained, creating a \textbf{bilingual mirror index}: a farmer querying in English can
surface knowledge originating exclusively in a Urdu lecture, and vice versa.

\textit{Stage 5 — Semantic Chunking.}
LangChain's \texttt{RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter} segments text into 1000-token chunks
with 200-token overlap. Separators are language-aware, including the Urdu sentence
terminator (۔), preventing agronomic instructions from splitting across chunk boundaries.

\textit{Stages 6--7 — Embedding and Qdrant Upsertion.}
Each chunk is independently embedded using OpenAI's \texttt{text-embedding-3-small} model
(1536-dimensional cosine space). Language detection is applied per-chunk to ensure accurate
metadata. Chunks are upserted into the Qdrant \texttt{pqnk\_v2} collection with rich
payloads: document name, category, language, chunk index, and source path.

\textit{Stages 8--10 — Persistence and Notification.}
A PostgreSQL record is committed capturing file metadata, uploader identity, Google Drive URL,
chunk count, and indexing status, followed by an asynchronous completion notification.

\subsection{Hybrid RAG Chatbot Engine}

\textit{Query Preprocessing.}
Each query undergoes spell correction (\texttt{PySpellChecker}) with a protected agricultural
vocabulary (PQNK, NPK, vermicompost, neem, rabi, kharif), entity and acronym resolution,
and intent normalisation.

\textit{Hybrid Retrieval.}
The query is embedded and searched against Qdrant via cosine similarity. For Urdu-script
queries, the query is additionally translated to English and searched in parallel;
results are merged and deduplicated, enabling true cross-lingual retrieval. For complex
analytical queries (``why'', ``how'', ``impact''), a multi-hop retrieval pass enriches
context with causally related passages. Query expansion via GPT-4o-mini paraphrasing
generates surface-form variants to compensate for varying levels of agricultural literacy.
All chunks are filtered by a minimum similarity threshold ($\theta = 0.25$), discarding
marginally relevant passages.

\textit{Domain-Restricted Generation.}
The top-$k$ ($k = 8$) chunks are injected into a structured system prompt:

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Domain-restriction system prompt (abbreviated).}]
You are an expert assistant for PQNK natural
farming by Dr. Asif Sharif. Answer ONLY from
the provided Context. If the answer is absent,
say "I don't have specific information on this
in the PQNK knowledge base." Never fabricate.
Cite your sources by document name.
\end{lstlisting}

GPT-4o generates responses at temperature 0.2. This strict grounding is the primary
mechanism for eliminating hallucination.

\textit{Bilingual Audio Output.}
Responses are synthesised using Microsoft Edge TTS neural voices: \texttt{ur-PK-UzmaNeural}
(Urdu) and \texttt{en-US-AriaNeural} (English), selected automatically by language
detection. The audio output lowers literacy and digital access barriers for rural users.

\textit{Conversation Memory.}
A sliding-window memory retains the last four conversation turns, and the most recently
referenced crop entity is preserved to resolve anaphoric references across turns
(e.g., ``What irrigation does \textit{it} need?'').

\subsection{Role-Based Access Control}

The system enforces a five-tier role hierarchy (Seeker, Farmer, Researcher, Admin,
Super Admin) at both the React frontend route level and Flask API decorator level.
Admins may upload and categorise resources; Super Admins manage users and role assignments.
Registration uses dual-channel OTP verification via Gmail SMTP and Twilio SMS.

\subsection{HCI Interface, Presentability, and Research Process}
The frontend interface was designed with a strong emphasis on Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) principles to ensure seamless usability for individuals with varying levels of digital literacy, particularly rural farmers. The UI employs a clean, distraction-free React-based architecture featuring distinct visual affordances, high-contrast typography, and intuitive navigation loops. Beyond mere aesthetic presentability, the interface is deeply integrated into the overarching research process: it actively prompts users to query specific agricultural methodologies, validates their understanding through audio-visual feedback loops, and captures their interaction pathways to incrementally refine the RAG engine's generation accuracy. This systematic integration ensures the platform is not only an informational repository but an active participant in the ongoing sustainable farming research ecosystem.

\section{Experiments and Results}

\subsection{Retrieval Quality}

To rigorously evaluate how well the chatbot’s responses align with verified reference answers, we developed a composite accuracy metric inspired by established methodologies in natural language processing literature. While existing studies often isolate either semantic similarity, keyword coverage, or hallucination penalties as independent metrics, we synthesized all three into a unified evaluation formula. To facilitate this, we manually constructed an evaluation suite of question-answer pairs derived directly from the source documents, comparing the ground-truth answers against the bot's generations. 

In our formulation, semantic similarity is intentionally heavily weighted (0.88) because capturing the exact contextual meaning and safety of agricultural advice must be prioritized over mere lexical overlap. This similarity is computed by comparing the \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2} embeddings of the generated responses against the actual answers. Keyword coverage acts as a vital supplementary weight (0.12), matching generated text against a custom-built agronomic term-bank to ensure the inclusion of critical domain terminology. Finally, a hallucination penalty is subtracted to rigidly penalize out-of-domain fabrications. The final accuracy score is formally defined as:

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Final Accuracy} = \max \Big( 0,\; 
& (0.88 \times \text{Semantic Similarity}) \\
& + (0.12 \times \text{Keyword Coverage}) \\
& - \text{Hallucination Penalty} \Big)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

This weighted formulation prioritizes semantic similarity over keyword coverage, reflecting the importance of capturing the underlying meaning of a response rather than relying solely on lexical overlap. This is particularly important in natural language generation tasks, where correct answers may be expressed using diverse phrasing.

The metric is applied to each question--answer pair, and the resulting scores are aggregated into a structured DataFrame. Both individual scores and the overall mean accuracy are reported to provide a comprehensive evaluation of the chatbot’s performance.

Semantic similarity is computed using cosine similarity between sentence embeddings generated by the \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2} model.
Retrieval was evaluated on 100 manually curated query-answer pairs derived from Dr.~Sharif's
verified documents and annotated by a PAR agronomist.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}

This paper presented the \textbf{PQNK Knowledge Intelligence System}, a full-stack AI
platform that addresses the knowledge-access gap facing Pakistani smallholder farmers.
By combining a fully automated multimodal ingestion pipeline, now covering documents
(with embedded image extraction), stand-alone images, video lectures, and audio
recordings, with a domain-restricted bilingual RAG chatbot, the system delivers
Dr.~Asif Sharif's expert agronomic guidance at scale while preserving the specificity
and internal consistency of PQNK principles.

Evaluation confirmed high retrieval precision (P@3 = 0.80), a low hallucination rate
(4\%), and sub-100ms semantic search latency at query time. Asynchronous ingestion
enables concurrent administrative uploads without disrupting the user-facing service,
and the bilingual mirror index enables true cross-lingual knowledge retrieval. The
newly introduced audio modality further expands corpus coverage, enabling private
expert voice recordings to silently enrich the knowledge base without cluttering the
public repository interface, a design pattern applicable to any domain where curated
expert knowledge must remain controlled.

We believe this system constitutes a replicable and scalable blueprint for expert
knowledge digitalisation across any low-resource, multilingual domain, wherever human
expertise is currently trapped in manual, unindexed formats.

\textbf{Future Work}:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep, leftmargin=*]

  \item \textbf{WhatsApp Bot Deployment.}
        Farmers across Pakistan rely on WhatsApp. Integrating the RAG engine with the Twilio
        WhatsApp API would allow users to query the system via voice note or text without
        requiring any internet literacy, directly addressing the rural digital divide.

  \item \textbf{Dedicated Urdu Vector Collection.}
        Current Urdu queries are translated to English before retrieval, incurring a
        8-point precision gap (Table~\ref{tab:retrieval}). Maintaining a parallel
        collection of Urdu-script embeddings using a multilingual encoder would close
        this gap without translation latency.

  \item \textbf{Offline-First Progressive Web App (PWA).}
        Rural farming communities frequently experience network interruptions. Converting
        the React frontend to a PWA with service worker caching would allow farmers to
        browse previously retrieved answers offline.

  \item \textbf{Continuous Alignment via User Feedback.}
        Embedding a feedback mechanism in the chatbot UI would accumulate a labelled
        preference dataset over time. This dataset could be used to progressively align
        response quality with the precise standards and idioms valued by the target community.

\end{enumerate}
