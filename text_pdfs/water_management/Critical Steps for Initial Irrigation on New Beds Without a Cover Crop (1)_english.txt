Critical Steps for Initial Irrigation on New Beds
Without a Cover Crop
PQNK - Natural Ecosystem Science for Production Agriculture
Farmers, as you transition to permanent raised beds, you have taken excellent steps by
levelling, breaking the hardpan, and forming beds. However, if you have skipped growing a
cover crop first, you will face a common challenge: water will not seep to the center of the
bed, and central seeds will not germinate.
This happens because without a cover crop, the soil lacks natural pores and channels for
water to move sideways. The soil is compacted and water moves very slowly through it.
Do not worry. This advisory provides a clear solution for this season and the permanent fix
for the future.
Immediate Solution for Your Current Crop:
1. Use Syphon Tubes for a Slow, Controlled Flow.
What to do: Always use syphon tubes to apply water in your furrows. Do not release a
large, fast flow of water.
Why: A fast flow of water runs off or only moves down quickly due to gravity, ignoring
the dry center. A slow flow from a syphon tube gives water time to be absorbed by the soil
and move sideways into the bed through capillary action.
2. Practice the Irrigate and Rest Method.
What to do: Irrigate until the furrows are full and you see water has seeped about 9-10
inches into the bed from each side. Then, stop the water completely. Wait for 2 full days
(48 hours). Then, irrigate again slowly using the syphon tubes. Repeat this process 2-3
times if needed.
Why: Stopping the irrigation allows the powerful suction force of the dry soil in the
center to actively pull moisture from the wet edges towards itself. Continuous
flooding kills this natural pulling action, waterlogs the roots, and can rot your seeds.
3. Check Moisture Correctly Before Planting.
What to do: Before you plant seeds in the center of the bed, dig a small hole in the
center down to the depth you will plant (2-3 inches). Feel the soil with your hand. It must
be moist. If it is dry, you need another Irrigate and Rest cycle.
Why: The surface can look dry even if there is moisture below, and vice-versa. Digging is
the only way to be sure the water has reached the exact place where your seeds need it to
germinate.
The Permanent Solution for All Future Seasons:
4. Never Skip the Cover Crop.
What to do: After forming your beds, always plant a cover crop like Janter. Let it grow,
then flatten it to form a thick mulch layer on your beds before planting your cash crop.
Why (The Real Reason): This is the most important step. The roots of the cover crop are
like natural drills. They create deep, permanent tunnels (pores) in your soil. These tunnels
act as pipelines for water and air, allowing water to seep across the entire bed quickly and
easily. This solves the seepage problem forever. The mulch also saves water by stopping
evaporation. This one step drastically reduces your future water, labour, and cost
problems.
Conclusion: Following the immediate steps will save your current crop. Investing in a
cover crop is an investment in your lands futureit will lower your costs and increase your
profits for years to come by building healthy, living soil.
