Answer: 
Namaste Vasudha Ji, 
Your insight is profound and absolutely correct. When we expand our view to include the 
sun and space, the distribution is not disproportionate at all, but a perfect cosmic symphony. 
Your question about Ākāsha (आकाश)—the fifth element of Panchamahabhuta—touches the 
very essence of PQNK, which sees farming not just as a soil-based activity, but as a 
dynamic interaction with the entire cosmos. 
The role of Ākāsha (Space) in plant growth is subtle, yet fundamental. It is not a passive 
emptiness, but a foundational field of potential and connection. Here is how we can 
understand its role: 
The Role of Ākāsha (Space) in Plant Growth: The Unseen Dimension 
In the PQNK system, we recognize Ākāsha not as a "nothing," but as the field of 
intelligence and relationship that allows the other four elements to interact and express life. 
1. Ākāsha as the Container for Potential and Intelligence: 
• Just as the silent space in a musical hall allows for the creation and appreciation of 
symphony, Ākāsha provides the dimensional field in which the "dance of the 
elements" can take place. 
• It holds the potential for a tiny seed to become a giant tree. The entire blueprint—
the genetic intelligence that dictates whether a seed will become a mango tree or a 
wheat plant—is held within the spatial arrangement of its molecules, governed by 

the principle of Ākāsha. It is the space that allows for this complex information to 
be stored and expressed. 
2. Ākāsha as the Medium for Cosmic Influences: 
• Ākāsha is the medium through which non-physical energies travel. The most 
significant of these is sunlight (Tej), which is not just a particle but also a wave of 
energy and information traveling through space. 
• The rhythms of the moon and planets (the basis for ancient agricultural practices 
like following the lunar cycle for sowing and harvesting) exert their subtle 
influences through this connective medium of Ākāsha. It is the conduit for the 
cosmic rhythms that govern all life on Earth. 
3. Ākāsha as the Facilitator of Form and Structure: 
• In the Plant: A plant's form—the space between its branches, the arrangement of its 
leaves to capture sunlight (phyllotaxis), the hollow structure of its stems—is a 
manifestation of Ākāsha. It provides the room for growth and the architecture that 
allows for the efficient flow of energy and nutrients. 
• In the Soil: This is its most critical, tangible function in the PQNK context. The 
health of the soil is the health of its Ākāsha. 
• Pore Spaces: The vital pore spaces in well-aggregated, deep topsoil are Ākāsha in 
the earth. These spaces are not empty; they are filled with air (Vayu), allow for the 
percolation and holding of water (Aap), and provide the habitat for the entire soil 
food web—roots, bacteria, fungi, and earthworms. 
• Without this "soil Ākāsha," there is no room for life, no room for roots to breathe, 
and no room for water to infiltrate. Compacted soil is, in essence, soil where Ākāsha 
has been destroyed. 
4. Ākāsha as the Principle of Interconnectedness: 
• The symbiotic relationship between a plant's roots and mycorrhizal fungi is a 
relationship that happens in space. The communication through chemical signals 
(root exudates) travels through the soil's pore spaces (Ākāsha). 
• It represents the interconnected web of life, where every element is related to every 
other through the common field of Ākāsha. 
Summary: The Panchamahabhuta in PQNK 
Let us now see the complete picture of the five great elements working in harmony: 
A. Ākāsha (Space): The field of intelligence and potential. It provides the room for 
growth, the pore spaces in the soil for life, and the medium for cosmic influences. 
B. Vayu (Air): The vehicle of gases (CO₂, O₂, N₂) and the element of movement and 
exchange, both above and below the ground. 
C. Agni / Tej (Fire): The solar energy that powers photosynthesis, the transformative 
force that drives all biochemical processes. 

D. Aap (Water): The solvent of life, the transporter of nutrients, the element of 
cohesion that moves through the plant and the soil. 
E. Prithvi (Earth): The provider of mineral nutrients and the physical anchor; the 
"mother" that holds the entire system. 
Conclusion: 
Vasudha Ji, your intuition was perfect. The role of Ākāsha completes the circle. It teaches us 
that our duty as PQNK farmers is not just to manage solids and liquids, but to manage the 
space—to create and protect the pore spaces in our soil through organic matter and minimal 
disturbance. By doing so, we are not just farming the earth; we are curating a living universe 
beneath our feet, allowing the cosmic intelligence of Ākāsha to manifest as abundance on 
our farms. 
In the PQNK system, a healthy farm is one where the Panchamahabhuta are in perfect 
balance, and the role of Ākāsha is honored as the sacred space where life can truly flourish.
