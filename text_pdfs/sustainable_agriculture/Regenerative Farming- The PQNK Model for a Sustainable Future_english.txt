The Role of Plants in Ecosystems
Plants as Lifes Foundation
Plants are uniquely designed to feed lifeforms
by converting sunlight, CO2, and minimal soil
nutrients into food.
They:
Absorb CO2, releasing oxygen and storing
carbon as food or stable soil carbon.
Rely on soil microbes to convert minerals into
usable nutrients (less than 10 of their total
needs).
Use water to transport nutrients and regulate
temperature via evaporation.

The Crisis of Industrial Agriculture
How Conventional Farming Disrupts Nature
Industrial farming practices like flood irrigation
and tillage cause:
Soil Degradation: Flooding drowns soil microbes,
reducing nutrient availability and food quality.
Habitat Destruction: Tillage demolishes microbial
habitats and root systems, creating hardpans that
restrict root growth and water infiltration.
Water Scarcity: Overuse of groundwater lowers
water tables (from 20 to 300 feet in some areas),
depleting aquifers.
Global Warming: Bare soil from tillage radiates
heat, contributing significantly to climate change.
Declining Food Quality: Over reliance on NPK
fertilizers ignores thousands of trace minerals,
lowering nutritional value over the past 50 years.

The PQNK Solution
Regenerative Practices for a Sustainable
Future
PQNK farmers adopt practices that align
with nature:
Breaking Hardpan: Prevents water
infiltration, root development, and soil
aeration.
Raised Beds: Prevent soil inundation,
preserve microbial life, and enhance nutrient
uptake for higher-quality food.
No-Till Farming: Protects soil structure,
retains water, and supports root growth,
reducing erosion and hardpan formation.
Carbon Sequestration: Healthy soils store
carbon, mitigating global warming.
Water Conservation: Improved soil
retention reduces reliance on groundwater,
addressing scarcity.
